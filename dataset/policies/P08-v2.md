# Records Retention Policy

Policy ID: P08
Version ID: P08-v2
Effective from: 2026-01-01
Supersedes: P08-v1

## Purpose

The purpose of the Records Retention Policy is to establish guidelines for the retention, access, and disposal of business records to ensure compliance with legal requirements and to promote efficient record management. This policy aims to protect sensitive information while facilitating the retrieval of necessary documents for operational needs.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party service providers who create, manage, or dispose of business records within the organization. It encompasses all forms of records, including electronic documents, emails, physical files, and any other data generated in the course of business operations.

## Requirements

All business records must be categorized according to their retention schedule, which specifies the duration for which each type of record must be kept. Employees are required to review their records quarterly and ensure that any documents nearing the end of their retention period are flagged for potential disposal or archiving, with a minimum retention period of three years for financial records and five years for employee records.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to the retention schedule may be granted with written approval from the department head or the Records Management Officer and must be documented in the records management system. In cases of ongoing litigation or audits, records must be retained until the matter is resolved, regardless of the standard retention period.

## Procedure

Employees must submit a request for record disposal to their immediate supervisor at least 14 days prior to the scheduled disposal date. Upon approval, the supervisor will forward the request to the Records Management Officer, who will oversee the secure disposal of the records in accordance with the established guidelines.

## Records

All records related to the retention and disposal of business documents must be maintained for a minimum of five years. This includes records of disposal requests, approvals, and any exceptions granted, which must be stored in a secure electronic format accessible only to authorized personnel.
